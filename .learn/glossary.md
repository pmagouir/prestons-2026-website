## Hard Voice Rules (Preston's anti-AI list — from preston-writing skill)

These are non-negotiable. Auditor flags every violation as Critical.

1. **No "equity" language anywhere on the site.** Use "accessibility," "impact," "barriers removed," "outcomes for FGLI students" instead. Hard rule from BRAIN/MEMORY.md.
2. **No "X, not Y" / "This isn't X — it's Y" / "Not X, but Y" constructions.** This is a high-confidence AI tell.
3. **No em-dash overuse.** Each page gets a budget — roughly one em-dash per 200 words. The current site overuses them; future drafts trim.
4. **No bullet-point prose.** A bullet list of single sentences strung together is not writing. Either the bullets carry distinct ideas or they are folded into paragraphs.
5. **Forbidden filler:** "genuinely," "honestly," "straightforward," "simply," "just," "leverages," "robust," "comprehensive solutions," "in today's landscape," "navigate complexity" (overused — flag if appears more than once per page).
6. **Forbidden tricolon for rhetorical emphasis** ("X, Y, and Z" three-part lists used only for cadence rather than substance).
7. **Active voice. Always.** Passive constructions tag a sentence for rewrite.
8. **Lead with the point.** Strategic context follows. Next move closes. This is the BLUF order Preston applies to every artifact.
9. **No elite-institution comparators.** Never frame DC CAP students against Ivy/Stanford/MIT.
10. **No "promotion ceiling" framing in Professional Inventory–adjacent content.** DC CAP context — promotion within is not the growth narrative.

## Approved Capability Tense Discipline

This is the website analog of dev-office's Pattern 4 (capability overstate). Apply on every claim.

| Status of capability | Tense / framing on site |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Operational today (Scholar Matching, AI Governance Framework, Salesforce instance) | Present tense. "I lead..." "We achieve..." |
| Building / scheduled (CPIP — Oct 2026 launch) | Future or progressive. "Building..." "Designing..." "Will launch October 2026..." |
| Past, completed | Past tense. "Led..." "Negotiated..." "Designed and implemented..." |

The site currently risks one specific failure: stating CPIP as a current capability. CPIP is a scheduled October 2026 soft launch. Until launch, frame as "building" or "designing," never "running" or "operating."

## Funder-Type Voice Distinction (consulting page only)

When writing about consulting services, register matches the buyer:

- **Foundation buyer:** mission-aligned, theory-of-change, outcomes language. Lead with population served.
- **Corporate buyer:** ROI, talent pipeline, workforce readiness. Lead with what the engagement produces.
- **Government / public-sector buyer:** compliance, scale, public benefit. Match the RFP register.
- **Individual leader / executive buyer:** personal, transformation. Lead with a specific outcome.

Currently the consulting page is undifferentiated. Resume Consultant should split CTAs / sub-blocks by buyer type during the next refresh.

## Writing Style Cues for Long-Form Content (writing/blog)

- 3-section essay structure: situation → friction → resolution. Not five-act, not academic.
- Opening hook is one sentence, ground-level concrete.
- One number-cite per ~300 words minimum (Preston's voice grounds in evidence).
- Short paragraphs. Two-to-four sentences. White space is part of the rhythm.
- One quoted operating principle per essay maximum, woven not headlined.

## Forbidden Self-Description

These phrases appear in AI-generated executive sites and must never appear on Preston's site:

- "Passionate about..."
- "Driven by..."
- "On a mission to..."
- "Deeply committed to..."
- "Building a future where..."
- "Believes in the power of..."
- "Bringing together a unique blend of..."

If a draft surfaces any of these, it is a sign the Resume Consultant slipped into AI-generic register. Auditor flags as Critical.
